Self-CTRL

Self-Consistency Training with Reinforcement Learning · MIT CSAIL, Jacob Andreas Lab · contributed talk at the Actionable Interpretability Workshop @ COLM 2026 · paper ↗

TL;DR

Language models explain their own behavior in ways that don't match what they actually do, which makes those explanations useless for auditing. Self-CTRL is a reinforcement-learning method that optimizes for consistency between a model's self-explanations and its behavior on related inputs — either updating the explanation to predict the behavior, or updating the behavior to match the explanation. On a Constitutional-AI refusal domain it raises a third-party auditor's ability to predict the model's refusals from 36% to 92% and cuts the HarmBench attack-success rate from 15.0% to 0.5%, without substantially increasing refusals on harmless prompts. Work with Itamar Pres, Laura Ruis, Belinda Z. Li, and Jacob Andreas.

The problem

If you want to audit a language model, one natural thing to do is ask it to explain itself. But a model's stated reasoning is often not a faithful account of how it actually acts — it can describe one decision rule while following another. That gap makes self-explanation unreliable exactly where it would be most useful: a third party trying to predict what the model will do from what it says about itself.

Method

Self-CTRL trains for self-consistency: the model's explanation of its own policy should predict its behavior on related inputs, and vice versa. The training signal is bidirectional — consistency can be achieved either by revising the explanation to match behavior or by revising the behavior to match the explanation — and it is optimized with a policy-gradient objective. Crucially, the signal is the model's own agreement with itself, so no ground-truth labels for the "correct" explanation are required.

Results

On a probabilistic-reasoning task (imitating biased samplers), Self-CTRL raises the correlation between the model's self-reported bias and its actual behavioral bias from R² = 0.24 to R² = 0.64 on held-out distributions — matching the generalization of direct ground-truth supervision, without using those labels.

On a Constitutional-AI refusal domain, it raises a third-party auditor's accuracy at predicting the model's refusals from 36% to 92%. As a safety side effect of that alignment, the HarmBench attack-success rate drops from 15.0% to 0.5% — a 30× reduction — without substantially raising refusals on harmless prompts.

Why it matters

Making a model's self-account match its behavior turns self-explanation into something an external auditor can actually rely on, and it does so using the model's own consistency rather than expensive labeled supervision. Full method, experiments, and appendices are in the paper, Self-CTRL: Self-Consistency Training with Reinforcement Learning, presented as a contributed talk at the Actionable Interpretability Workshop @ COLM 2026.
